BENV0093 Coursework #

  • Title: Renewable Energy Power Station Investment Using Spatial and Multi-Criteria Decision-Making Analysis

  • Deadline: Wednesday, 6 January 2027, at 11:00 a.m.

  • Submission Format: 3,000 words (+5% margin). A link to the Python code must be included at the end of the report.

Project Context#

According to the Sixth Carbon Budget (Climate Change Committee, 2020), by 2050 the UK is expected to consume more than twice the amount of electricity used today. Meeting this demand will require approximately four times more clean energy generation and a doubling of grid capacity. To stay on track for net zero, around 10 GW of new renewable capacity must be developed each year.

One of the largest existing onshore wind farms in the UK — the Clyde Wind Farm in Scotland — currently operates 206 turbines with an installed capacity of 0.5 GW. This provides a useful benchmark for understanding the scale of the challenge. To meet annual renewable expansion targets, the UK would effectively need to deliver the equivalent of 20 Clyde-sized onshore wind farms each year. Wind and solar are now the cheapest forms of electricity generation, with energy from gas-fired power stations costing nearly three times as much to produce. Expanding renewable capacity will therefore not only accelerate decarbonisation but also help to reduce energy bills and enhance energy security.

In this project, you will assume the role of a spatial planning consultant advising the UK National Grid Strategy Team (or an equivalent governmental or industry body). Your task is to identify and justify suitable locations for 20 onshore wind farms across the UK, each with an installed capacity of approximately 0.5 GW (500 MW). Collectively, these projects would add around 10 GW of new renewable capacity to the national energy mix.

1. Research Questions#

This project will require you to present relevant data and analysis concerning the current state of the UK’s energy profile, its policy framework, and decarbonisation ambitions. You will also be expected to process and visualise spatial datasets to support your analysis, provide an evidence-based set of recommendations, and deliver a final strategic report for the National Grid Strategy Team. By the end of your report, you should be able to outline a coherent and realistic onshore wind farm development strategy that can contribute to the UK’s target of expanding renewable electricity capacity by 2050. In particular, your report should answer the following key research questions:

  • 1) What is the current spatial distribution of onshore renewable power stations across the UK, and what patterns emerge from their location and scale?

  • 2) Where would you recommend developing 20 new onshore wind farms, each with an installed capacity of approximately 0.5 GW, to achieve an additional 10 GW of renewable capacity?

  • 3) Is investing in additional onshore wind farms a financially viable strategy for meeting the UK’s 2050 renewable electricity targets? Would you recommend such a strategy even if it requires a subsidy?

    Students should assess the economic feasibility of developing 20 new onshore wind farms (each 0.5 GW) by estimating indicators such as Net Present Value (NPV) and Levelised Cost of Electricity (LCOE). The analysis should account for the expected lifespan, capital and operational costs (optional, to determine whether large-scale onshore wind development would be a profitable or subsidy-dependent investment in the long term.

From speed to power#

def calc_turbine_speed(speed, turbine_height=70, data_height=10, hellman_exponent=1/7):
    turbine_speed = speed * (turbine_height / data_height) ** hellman_exponent
    return turbine_speed

# Discrete power curve: for wind speeds 0 to 24 m/s
power_curve = np.array([
    0, 0, 0, 102, 257, 471, 750, 1194, 1713, 2227, 2465, 2500,
    2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2500, 2400, 2250
])

wind_speeds = np.arange(0, 25)

def speed_to_power(speed_array):
    speed_array = np.clip(speed_array, 0, 24)  # avoid out-of-bounds
    return np.interp(speed_array, wind_speeds, power_curve)

Net present value (NPV)#

def calc_NPV(annual_revenue, lifetime_yrs, CAPEX, dis=0.05, OPEX=0):

    t = np.arange(1, lifetime_yrs + 1)
    discounted_cash_flows = (annual_revenue - OPEX) / (1 + dis) ** t #annual netflow
    NPV = discounted_cash_flows.sum() - CAPEX
    print(discounted_cash_flows.sum())
    return round(NPV, 0)

Levelized cost of energy (LCOE)#

LCOE stands for Levelized Cost of Electricity, a metric used to measure the average cost to build and operate a power plant over its lifetime, expressed as a cost per unit of electricity generated (usually USD/MWh). It’s a way to compare the cost-effectiveness of different energy generation methods by accounting for both initial and ongoing expenses over a project’s full lifespan.

def calc_LCOE(capex, opex, annual_generation, lifetime, discount_rate):

    years = np.arange(1, lifetime + 1)

    # discount factors
    discount_factors = 1 / (1 + discount_rate) ** years

    # discounted totals
    total_discounted_cost = capex + np.sum(opex * discount_factors)
    total_discounted_generation = np.sum(annual_generation * discount_factors)
    
    lcoe = total_discounted_cost / total_discounted_generation
    return round(lcoe, 2)
